# PEOPLE — kitchen

> Who's involved, what each needs, how to work with them. Example stream (fictional) — it also demonstrates the **people-rule**: record observable behaviour + impact as fact; label any read of *why* as a dated, provisional inference; keep it to conduct, never fixed character; write as if they'll read it.

## Jamie — worktop fitter

**Role / fact:** templates, cuts and fits the stone worktop. Came recommended; quote was fair; cabinet-paint job he saw he complimented.

**What he needs:** the handles decided before he drills; a clear run at the kitchen on fit day.

**Behaviour (fact):** booked 2026-05-26 for "this week"; on 2026-06-10 moved the date to 24 Jun, citing a stone-supplier delay. One slip so far.

**Read (inference, 2026-06-10 — provisional):** likely a genuine supplier issue rather than over-booking — he gave a specific cause and a firm new date. *Hold lightly; revisit if the 24th also moves.*

> **Why this entry is shaped this way (the teaching point):** the easy version would be "Jamie's unreliable / always late." That's a fixed-character verdict off one data point — exactly what the people-rule guards against. The factual version records *what happened* (one slip, with a stated cause) and labels the *why* as a dated guess to revisit. Same information, fair to Jamie, and it ages well: if the 24th holds, the inference closes positive; if it slips again, *then* there's a pattern worth noting — still as behaviour, not character.
